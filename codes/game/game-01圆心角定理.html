01 - Central Angle Theorem

📘 Basic Definition

Vertex at the center, both sides intersecting the circle, the angle is called a central angle.

A central angle is an angle whose vertex is the center of a circle.

📐 Theorem Content

In the same or congruent circles:

Equal central angles ⇔ Equal intercepted arcs

Equal central angles ⇔ Equal chords

📌 Important Corollary

If any one pair (angle, arc, chord) is equal, the other two are equal.

Key Insight: The measure of a central angle is equal to the measure of its intercepted arc.

🌍 Real-World Application

Engineers use this theorem when designing circular structures like bridges and domes. For example, calculating the angle for structural supports in a circular arch.

📜 Historical Context

First documented by Euclid in "Elements" (c. 300 BCE). This theorem is foundational for all circle geometry.

❓ Practice Problems

1. If the central angle is 90°, what is the measure of its intercepted arc?

2. Two central angles are congruent. What can you say about their intercepted arcs?

3. In a circle with radius 10 cm, a central angle of 120° intercepts an arc. Calculate the arc length.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

• Confusing central angles with inscribed angles

• Forgetting the theorem only applies to angles in the same circle

• Misidentifying the intercepted arc (it's the arc between the two points on the circle)
